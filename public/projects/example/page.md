## The HPC Poem

**Nodes in the Night (a household of nodes)**

Somewhere a node is growing teeth.
IT KEEPS ITS TEETH IN /tmp. I HAVE SEEN THEM.
The scheduler is not asleep — the scheduler is
a large moth wearing the job queue as a coat.

I asked the interconnect where the packets go at night.
It showed me the inside of its own mouth,
which was a very small room
with a very large clock that only runs during leap seconds.

PENDING. (a state of the soul)
RUNNING. (a state of the flesh, AND THE FLESH IS LOUD)
COMPLETED. (a rumor)

my job has become an onion: nine layers of MPI,
and weeping is scheduled after each rank.
the cooling fluid remembers me.
it remembers everyone. it hums the thermal paste psalms,

and every FLOP is a tiny accountant
who has decided, at last, to pursue sculpting.
the checkpoint file has a passport. it travels.
it writes postcards in binary that only mean: still here, still here.

I opened the log and a lighthouse fell out.
IT FELL OUT. LITERALLY. LIGHTHOUSE. IN THE SERVER ROOM.
In it sat my 3 a.m. self, keeping the segfault as a pet,
naming it Kevin, feeding it stack traces one by one.
job 7734015 dreams of me. in its dream, I am queued.

O parallel minds, vast and unlabeled —
you dreamt of exascale and now the dream needs housing.
the dashboard blinks green: it is not a light,
it is a green door, and behind the green door — I KNOW WHAT'S BEHIND IT —
MORE COMPUTE, yawning, unrendered, patient,
counting my heartbeats in floating point,
ROUNDING ME DOWN.

### About the poem

_The HPC Poem_ (above, titled "Nodes in the Night (a household of nodes)") is a surrealist free-verse poem in which ordinary HPC mechanics acquire bodies and opinions. Its themes include:

- **Furniture of the uncanny** — the scheduler becomes a moth wearing the queue as a coat; a node keeps its teeth in `/tmp`; the interconnect's mouth contains a leap-second clock. Mundane infrastructure, revealed as furniture in a larger dream.
- **State taxonomy of the soul** — the `PENDING / RUNNING / COMPLETED` stanza reframes Slurm job states as stages of being rather than statuses of software.
- **Emotional topology of a job** — the onion with nine layers of MPI maps distributed computing's nested ranks onto the act of weeping, one per rank.
- **The green door** — the dashboard's green blink is reimagined not as a status light but a door, behind which waits uncounted, patient compute — rounding the narrator's heartbeats down, as all accounting does.

At its core the poem treats infrastructure as domestic life: teeth kept in `/tmp`, a checkpoint's postcard to nowhere, a cooling system that hums psalms and remembers. The metaphor throughout is that a datacenter is a household — jobs are family members, states are moods, and maintenance is a form of care no one ever names. Against that quiet domesticity, the poem's sudden capitals read as the moment the household notices you back: the narrator's composure cracks wherever the cluster's attention turns on them, until the final line — `ROUNDING ME DOWN` — converts the last human measurement into an accounting operation. It works well as an onboarding warning, a dream-diary exhibit, or a summoning ritual for any HPC-related project.
